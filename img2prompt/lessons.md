# Lessons for Krea 2 V2 + Natural Raw 2

What earlier images taught about getting this model and preset to match a
reference. Every writer reads this before its first prompt, and adds to it
when its image is finished. General rules only — nothing about one
particular subject. Review it now and then and move what holds up into
base_prompt.md.

- The default negative's "faded colors, pastel tones, beigesthetic, flat lighting" plus phone-processing words ("computational sharpening") push renders into an over-saturated, HDR, crunchy look; drop those terms and ask for "plain, unedited, low contrast, muted natural colour, no HDR/sharpening" — this was the largest single jump (photo character 2→7).
- Camera height barely responds to wording: "eye level", "level view, not from below" and low-angle/selfie negatives all still gave a low, selfie-like angle, so don't spend revisions on it. Face-fullness words overshoot: "plump/rounded cheeks" or "broad smile" makes the face chubby and pulls heavy liner and gloss back in, while "pointed chin/longer oval" makes it too narrow; change face shape in small steps.
- Colour names drift: "sage" alone went mint, grey or khaki between iterations, and "warm amber pine" went glossy orange-red. Pin a colour with "X, not Y, not Z" exclusions, and for wood add "matte, unvarnished, non-reflective".
- Material and size modifiers overshoot: "cotton-silk sheen" became a textured ribbed silk in a different colour, and "medium gold chain" became a chunky curb chain. Keep fabrics plain ("matte cotton") and jewellery small ("thin chain"). Saturated colour names swing between hot and pastel, so anchor the target with exclusions on both sides.
- Age and face-fullness words ("around 30", "early 30s", "fuller cheeks", "rounded jaw", with "teenage/V-jaw" in the negative) did not move the face: it stayed early-20s, slim and sharp-jawed. Don't spend many revisions on it. Also change only one or two things per revision; a many-change rewrite lost wins that were already in place (hair length).
- Blended or "X-leaning" colour names collapse to one side: "yellow-leaning pistachio-sage" became butter-yellow and "grey-green" became slate. A plain common colour name plus a concrete object ("light olive green, the soft green of dried sage leaves") with exclusions on both sides is what landed a muted colour.
- Unspecified lighting and makeup default to dim warm lamp light and glam makeup (blush, wet highlighter, coloured lids, glossy lips). Spell out "bright even daylight, whites render white" and "no blush, no highlighter, no eyeshadow" in the prompt, and put those terms in the negative too. That fixed both in one step. The model also adds a bindi to South Asian subjects; add "bindi" to the negative when the reference has none.
- "Curvy" and "full upper arms/thighs" did not stop body build from rendering slimmer than the reference, the same way face-fullness words fail.
- Z-Image Turbo i2i refine of a finished Krea render (Default preset, LoRAs off, 12 steps, CFG 1, er_sde, same prompt): use denoise 0.25. Over 8 renders it kept the content every time and looked more real in 7 (pores, fabric texture, less airbrushed), about +0.2 to +0.5. 0.35 redrew the face, print or jewellery in 2 of 4; 0.45 changed content in 4 of 4. It only fixes realism: colour, face shape, lighting and pose errors in the Krea render carry through. It can make chest skin blotchy or warm the colour, so compare it with the Krea original before keeping it.
- Left/right placement mirrors unless each side is named from the viewer's side: "her left hand (on the image right)" fixed a mirrored pose in one step (pose 4→7). Accessories can still swap wrists, so say which hand has each one, say what the other wrist does not have, and add the wrong placement to the negative.
- Print-size words overshoot like other modifiers: "thin pencil-line pinstripes" rendered as a faint texture with no stripes, and "finger-width bold stripes" as thick tiger bands. Give a middle size and compare it to a familiar object.
- "Muted natural colour, low contrast" with a dark foliage background drifts into a dim, desaturated, matte film grade. Once that happens, ask for "bright exposure, luminous face, natural fairly saturated colour" and put "matte film grade, desaturated, underexposed" in the negative.
- Softening a face can tip Krea into a teenage look: "fuller soft oval, rounded chin", heavy lids, bare face and a dim soft phone-video look together made img6's render read as under 18 (the render was deleted and the image stopped). When making a face fuller or removing makeup, keep explicit adult cues in the same sentence: the adult age range, mature bone structure, adult proportions; never rely on "teenager" in the negative to do it.
- With a white wall or pillar in frame, Krea adds a bright vignette, bloom and glowing white edges, and "no vignette" in the photography specs did not remove it. A "warm ~3800K" light can turn into a yellow tungsten cast from one seed to the next even when the wording stays the same. Pin the colour with a neutral Kelvin value and "whites render neutral off-white, not yellow".
- "Head tilted toward the pillar/wall" makes the model rest the head on it and grip it with both hands. Give the gap ("head a hand's width from the pillar") and say where each hand is. "Glossy lipstick" or "dewy skin" also spreads a wet hotspot onto the cheeks, so add "matte foundation, no shine on the cheeks" in the same sentence.
- Shot width does not respond to wording: "medium-wide", "lots of headroom", "top fifth empty wall", "middle 60% of the width", "knee-up three-quarter length", "4 m away", "small in the frame", with "close-up, tight crop, head at top edge" in the negative, still gave a tight crop with the head near the top and the body filling the frame. Like camera height, don't spend revisions on it.
- Smile size swings between extremes: "big open smile showing upper teeth" gave a full toothy grin, and "soft smile, sliver of teeth" gave a closed-lip smirk. Describe a medium smile ("a small row of upper teeth") and name both extremes as exclusions. "Heather grey" renders as a light, strongly marled slub knit; say "flat smooth mid-grey jersey, almost no heather texture".
- Necklines plunge regardless of wording: sweetheart/bodycon dresses rendered as a deep V with a larger bust in every render. Neither "high sweetheart, a hand's width below the collarbone", "straight-across, not a V", "four fingers below the collarbones" nor dropping the default negative's anatomy block (breasts/bra/genital terms) fixed it, and naming "cleavage" or "plunge" in either field makes it worse. Don't spend revisions on it. A rewrite aimed at the neckline ("structured strapless-style bodice") also turned the midi dress into a mini and the kneeling pose into a frontal sit, so keep proven pose/dress wording verbatim.
- Jewellery size words flip to the extreme: "chunky chain-link, wider than a tennis chain" gave a heavy Cuban link; "fine/delicate linked stones" gave the right thin chain. Big smiles ("small row of upper teeth") narrow the eyes into a squint; say the eyes stay wide open. The giant "2" foil balloon was rendered as a "7" when the text didn't insist on it ("a curved 2, clearly not a 7").
- Naming hips, butt or bust (even "full", "natural", "in proportion", "modest") makes Krea pinch the waist into an hourglass and inflate those parts. For a thick or full-figured build, describe only the overall shape ("sturdy, stocky, straight-sided rectangle-to-apple figure, torso nearly straight from ribcage to waistband, thick soft midsection, thick soft upper arms, one evenly thick silhouette, nothing lifted or emphasised"), never name those parts, and put hourglass/curvy/cinched waist/big butt/large bust in the negative. That gave the best build score. The bust still rendered too large and forward even with "loose top hanging flat".
- "Hazy" or "open shade" lighting words turn into a dull amber, golden-hour grey; "hard, clear midday sun, no haze" with a named rim gave crisp light. Round, "apple-cheek" face wording plus a bare face made the face read about 20; restating "mature grown woman of about 28, adult bone structure" in the face sentence pulled the age back up.
- Thick/full-figured references: "a thick mommy figure" works where every anatomical wording failed (img 843404960: renders C4/D4 judged good by the user, after "full hips and butt" pinched the waist and inflated the butt, and pawg LoRA, with or without its SWWH trigger, changed nothing visible). Two forms both worked: (C) "Body: a thick mommy figure, a soft, full-figured mature mom body: a thick soft waist with almost no taper and a slight soft belly, wide soft hips and full thick thighs, thick soft fleshy upper arms, a soft heavy everyday build; a modest chest under the loose top." with wide-hips/big-butt dropped from the negative; (D) "Body: a thick mommy figure; " prepended to an overall-shape sentence (sturdy, straight-sided rectangle/apple figure, thick soft middle, thick soft upper arms) with hourglass/curvy/big-butt kept in the negative. Keep the mature adult age cues in the same sentence. Results vary by seed, so judge across iterations.
- Light colour drags the wall colour with it: warm golden sunlight turned an emerald wall pale sage, and "flat overcast, neutral" with "deep forest green" made it too dark and contrasty. "Soft open-shade daylight, ~5000K" with a medium saturated green plus exclusions on both sides (not neon, not dark forest, not sage) got closest. Gaze words also overshoot: "glancing up and away" gave an upward look with a closed mouth; "glance sideways toward image right, at about eye level, not upward" fixed it.
- Hair tint and skin depth are binary: "near-black, faint reddish sheen, not auburn" removed the tint entirely while "subtle burgundy tint" earlier gave all-over red. "Medium-brown Indian complexion, not fair, not pale" still rendered fairer on the face in neutral light; only warm golden light came close, so expect to need a stronger "deeper warm brown" and repeat it for the face, arms and legs.
- Arm and prop placement moves when each arm is named by its frame side and the wrong spot is ruled out: "the arm on the image-left side crosses in front of her belly ... the mitt is at her waist, nowhere near her chest" moved a held object off the chest in one step (pose 3→7). Downcast eyes render closed unless the prompt says "eyes clearly open, not closed, irises visible". "Black curly hair" goes jet black, in tight ringlets falling forward over the chest. Give the shade ("very dark brown, not jet black") and say where the hair falls ("behind the shoulder, down the back, not over the chest").
- Form C "thick mommy figure" with "heavy ... hang low" bust wording overshoots into an oversized, low-hanging bust. A cup size plus height ("about a C cup, sits at mid-chest, moderate sag, not oversized") pulled it back while the rest of the thick build stayed.
- Body-hair amount is binary like hair tint: "natural dark untrimmed pubic hair" (with the term dropped from the negative) still rendered nearly bare, while "full natural bush, thick patch" covered the genitals entirely. Ask for a moderate patch and say what must stay visible. Words like "crisp, sharp professional detail" and "high-key flash" bring in an oversharpened, crunchy HDR skin texture, and naming sharpening at all, even "mild sharpening only", made it worse. Ask for "natural unprocessed studio sharpness".
- Mustard/ochre fabric swings like the other saturated colours: "ochre-mustard tan" went orange-brown, "golden mustard-yellow" went lemon, and "pale mustard like wheat" went beige. Write "muted dusty mustard-tan" with exclusions on both sides. "Feet clearly separated" and "ankles crossed" both moved foot placement, so pose words for the feet do work.
- Bust-shrinking words spread to the whole body: adding "B cup, small-to-medium, not large, not heavy" to a thick-mommy body sentence turned the build slim and toned. Adding "chubby, clearly plump and not slim, no muscle tone" to the body sentence, with slim/toned/flat belly/defined waist in the negative, brought the thick build back, but the bust then grew again. Change bust and build wording in separate revisions. Nude subjects still got a bracelet or ring with only "no jewellery"; spell out "bare wrists, no bracelet, bare fingers, no rings" and put ring/bracelet in the negative.
- Leg placement in a straddle did not respond: "thigh flat and level, horizontal across the left third, not angled down" still rendered sloping to the bottom corner in 3 of 3 renders, so don't spend revisions on it. Describing an arm "angled down and outward to the lower right" along with a camera pulled back made the model lean the whole torso forward into a hunch; keep a proven upright-torso sentence word for word when you change the arms or framing.
- Scene mode: check build, outfit type and the act against the reference before writing, because writers and judges both misread them here (very wide hips and butt read as "full", a pulled-aside bra and panties read as a swimsuit, the act left implicit). Name the hip/butt width outright when the lower body is much wider than a slim top, and state the act in plain words.
- Pushed-aside underwear: "lingerie bra and panties ... pulled aside" rendered as a bikini or sheer set covering the breasts in 2 of 2 renders. Put the bare state first in the prompt ("topless, both breasts fully bare, bra pulled down into a band beneath both breasts") and covered breasts/bikini/sheer fabric in the negative. That bared the breasts, but naming them up front made them render larger, so put the size cue ("small, flat-lying, low projection") in the same opening sentence.
- An open-placket shirt neckline with an inner layer can be fixed where plunging necklines can't: "only the top two buttons undone, inside the V a plain black high-necked fitted top fills the whole opening, starting just below the necklace" removed cleavage in one step (bust 4→7). Slimming words in the same revision ("longer oval, narrower lower face, narrower torso") made the whole person slim and younger, so change the neckline alone.
- Hair side swaps unless both sides are spelled out: "almost all the hair swept to the image-right side, over her left shoulder ... on the image-left side tucked behind the shoulder, bare shoulder, no hair down that side" fixed it in one step. Lip colour words swing like fabric colours: "nude-pink" went coral and "mauve-nude/brown-rose" went mauve-brown. Even with "no blush", raised contrast and saturation give the cheeks a pink-red glow, so ask for low contrast and low saturation along with the no-blush wording.
- Shot width does move with structure, not wording: three revisions of "3 m away, half the frame height" kept a close crop, but opening the prompt with the whole setting and "a wide street snapshot from 3–4 m back", then placing her as "a small figure in the middle third of the frame, head to mid-calf, the sidewalk in front of her", with the lower body (skirt to mid-calf, the ground) described concretely, the face cut to one line, and "portrait crop, waist-up, medium shot, cropped at the thighs" in the negative, took pose_framing from 4 to 6 in one step. Put the framing first and keep face detail short when the reference is a wide shot.
- Shape words overshoot like colour and size words: "stepped, curved bar ends" became a full zigzag/staircase grille. Describe the main run first ("straight and level for most of its length") and the detail as one small exception. "Slightly less purple than magenta" rendered as magenta-purple, because naming the colour you want to avoid pulls toward it; name the warm side ("watermelon pink leaning toward red") and put purple/fuchsia in the exclusions instead.
- Two-person shots mirror the couple (who stands on which side) unless it is stated outright: "Placement, named from the viewer's side: the man is on the image-LEFT, the woman on the image-RIGHT; not mirrored", with "mirrored composition, man on the right" in the negative, fixed it in one step. A person leaning on a swing plank or rail renders seated on it unless the prompt says "standing upright, not sitting on it, legs straight and vertical, feet on the ground, the plank behind her upper thighs".
- With a plank or rail in frame, hands drift onto it: "neither of her hands touches the plank" still got both hands gripping the log, and "arms wrap around her waist" from a person behind became hands on her hips. Expect to spend revisions on hand placement in hug poses. A colour exclusion can flip to the other extreme: "royal blue, not navy" fixed a near-black navy but then a true navy became bright royal, so anchor the target ("deep navy, not black, not bright royal blue").
- Draped garments (saree pallu, shawl, sash) render mirrored whatever shoulder name is used: "her LEFT shoulder (image right)", "IMAGE RIGHT side" and "hangs from the image-right shoulder" all put it on the other side over 4 iterations. Describing it by where it sits in the frame worked: "the left half of the photo shows plain blouse, no fabric on it; the right half is covered by the panel; its edge forms one diagonal like a forward slash '/' from upper right to lower left". The panel then came out narrow and the midriff still showed, so also give its width and say it covers the waist. "No bare midriff" plus belly/navel in the negative did not stop a saree from baring the waist.
- A broad toothy smile narrows the eyes into a squint, and swapping to "calm gentle smile" with "thin kajal" gave a slimmer face with heavy smoky kohl. Keep the proven smile wording and add "eyes wide open while she smiles". Any kajal or liner wording drifts toward heavy kohl, so write "faint, at the upper lash roots only, no smudged kohl".
- Brow thickness swings like other size words: "thick, dark, full brows" gave heavy straight bushy brows, and "medium-thickness, pencil line at the tail" gave thin light brows. Ask for the middle ("medium-thick, full at the inner ends, tapering at the tail") and exclude both ends. Earring size words do the same: "about the size of a fingertip" gave large bright-yellow hoops, while "tiny huggie, no bigger than a pea, tight against the lobe, muted antique gold, not bright yellow" fixed size and colour in one step.
